Ford Motor Co.: one notch from the line

A fundamental credit review of Ford's automotive business after a bruising 2025 and a 2026 rebound. The balance sheet is strong. The rating cushion is not.

Summary

Credit view

Stable fundamentals · Market Weight

Ford's automotive balance sheet is investment-grade quality. It has $46.4B of liquidity against $23.7B of debt, no financial maintenance covenants, and a light maturity schedule through 2030. The weak point is earnings. FY2025 automotive margins fell below the level Moody's has said would trigger a downgrade, and S&P has rated Ford BBB- with a negative outlook since February 2025. A 2026 recovery, now guided to $10.5–11.0B of adjusted EBIT, should stabilize the rating. But one more cut by S&P would leave Ford with only one investment-grade rating. That would require subsidiary guarantees for its bank lenders, ranking those lenders ahead of today's bondholders.

Business overview

Ford is a Dearborn, Michigan automaker with $187.3B of FY2025 revenue. Since 2023 it has reported three operating segments plus its captive finance arm:

  • Ford Pro: commercial vehicles and services (Transit, Super Duty, fleet software). It is the most profitable segment and the core of the credit story, with $6.8B of EBIT in FY25.
  • Ford Blue: gas and hybrid retail vehicles (F-150, Bronco, Explorer). It is a cash generator, but exposed to warranty costs, recalls and tariffs. FY25 EBIT was $3.0B, down from $7.5B in FY23.
  • Ford Model e: electric vehicles and software. It has lost about $5B a year since FY23, and the December 2025 EV reset is designed to shrink that loss.
  • Ford Credit: the captive lender that funds dealer inventory and consumer loans and leases. It is profitable (FY25 earnings before tax of $2.6B) and carries most of the consolidated debt. That debt is self-funded and secured by its own receivables.

Credit analysis of an automaker with a captive lender has to split the two. Ford Credit runs at about 10x debt to equity, which is normal for a finance company. Its $139B of debt is backed by its own loan book, not by the industrial business. Folding it into consolidated leverage would make Ford look far more indebted than it is. The industrial credit is the Company business on its own, and that is what this memo rates.

Operating performance

FY2025 was the stress test. Adjusted EBIT fell 34% to $6.8B (a 3.6% margin), and net income swung to a $8.2B loss after $17.4B of special items. Three things went wrong at once:

  • Tariffs. They cost about $2B of net EBIT in FY25, and Ford suspended its guidance in May 2025. Following court rulings on the emergency-powers (IEEPA) tariffs, Ford now expects about $2.2B in reimbursements and had received about $0.5B by Q3 2026.
  • Novelis aluminum fires (Sept and Nov 2025). Two fires at a supplier's plant cost about $2B of EBIT through lost F-Series production. Ford expects a net $1B recovery in 2026.
  • EV reset (Dec 2025). Ford took an $8.4B Model e impairment, cancelled three EV programs and dissolved its BlueOval SK battery joint venture with SK On, ending a $6.6B capital commitment. Most of the charge is non-cash, and it lowers future cash burn.

The rebound has started. Q3 2026 adjusted EBIT was $2.7B (a 5.4% margin), and on 28 October Ford raised FY2026 guidance for the third time, to $10.5–11.0B adjusted EBIT and $6–7B adjusted free cash flow. Segment guidance is Ford Pro about $7.0B, Ford Blue about $5.5B, Model e about –$4.0B and Ford Credit about $2.8B. Ford Pro has carried the company. Even in FY25 it produced more EBIT than the whole automotive business did.

Company debt is mostly long-dated senior unsecured notes, $17.1B at YE2025. Examples include the 3.25% notes due 2032, the 4.75% notes due 2043 and the 5.291% notes due 2046. Coupons are low and interest cost is modest at $1.25B in FY25. Ford Motor Co. itself has not issued public notes since 2022. New borrowing has come from a $3B delayed-draw term loan (undrawn) and a $3.8B Department of Energy (DOE) loan at 4.814%, taken over from the battery joint venture in May 2026. Interest only is due on that loan until 2030.

The DOE loan explains most of the 2026 increase in debt. Together with a lower cash balance, it is why net cash has fallen from $12.4B to $1.6B in under four years. Ford's real credit buffer is now its liquidity, not a large net cash pile.

By the usual leverage and coverage measures, Ford reads as a solid BBB credit. Even the FY25 trough left debt/EBITDA at 2.3x and coverage above 7x. Margin is the binding constraint. Rating agencies weight profitability heavily for automakers because the industry is cyclical and capital-intensive, and a 2–5% EBIT margin leaves little room for the next shock.

Liquidity & maturities

Liquidity is the strongest part of the credit. At Q3 2026, Company liquidity was $46.4B: $25.3B of cash plus $23.6B of committed, undrawn credit lines, less restricted cash. The lines include a $13.5B corporate revolver, a $2.0B supplemental revolver, a $2.5B 364-day facility and the $3.0B delayed-draw term loan. In April 2026 Ford extended the revolver, putting $10.1B of it to April 2031 and $3.4B to April 2029.

  • No financial maintenance covenants, such as leverage, coverage or net-worth tests, and no rating triggers that would accelerate repayment.
  • The only covenant is a $4B minimum liquidity requirement. That is less than a tenth of current liquidity.
  • The maturity wall is light. After the $2.3B convertible note was repaid in Q1 2026, maturities are about $1.2B or less in each year from 2027 to 2030.

Cash flow & shareholder returns

Adjusted free cash flow (Ford's measure for the Company business) fell from $9.1B in FY22 to $3.5B in FY25. Shareholder distributions did not fall with it. Ford kept its regular dividend and paid out $3.0B in FY25, 85% of adjusted FCF, against its stated target of 40–50%. That is defensible for one trough year, given the cash balance. But it is a choice a creditor should watch. If FY26 free cash flow comes in near the $6–7B guide, the payout falls back to about 45%.

Why the S&P outlook matters more than usual

Ford's corporate credit facility says that if its senior unsecured debt loses its second investment-grade rating from Fitch, Moody's and S&P, certain subsidiaries must guarantee the facility. Moody's already rates Ford high yield, so a single S&P downgrade would set off the guarantees. Bank lenders would then have claims on subsidiary assets that today's bondholders do not, and the notes would become structurally subordinated to the revolver. Downgrade risk here means more than a move between index buckets. It changes where noteholders stand in a downside case.

Where the triggers sit

  • Moody's (Ba1): an upgrade needs an automotive EBIT margin sustained above 5%. A downgrade follows a margin below 4% or debt/EBITDA above 3x. FY25 was about 2.4% on my calculation, and FY26E is about 4.6%. Moody's affirmed Ford after the EV reset, which suggests it is looking through to the 2026 recovery.
  • S&P (BBB-, negative): it has pointed to an automotive EBITDA margin of about 8% as the downgrade threshold. My approximation puts FY25 at about 5.5% and FY26E at about 7.7%, close to the threshold but not yet back above it. S&P adjusts EBITDA for pensions and leases, so its figures will differ from mine.

My view: the split BBB-/Ba1 rating is about right. On current guidance, FY26 should satisfy Moody's 4% floor and approach S&P's 8% EBITDA margin. That makes an S&P move back to stable in 2027 more likely than a downgrade, provided warranty costs and tariffs do not take another $2B or more out of EBIT.

Strengths & risks

＋ Strengths

  • $46.4B of liquidity, about 2x Company debt, with revolvers extended to 2029–2031.
  • No maintenance covenants, only a $4B liquidity minimum.
  • Light maturities: about $1.2B or less a year from 2027 to 2030, with low-coupon, long-dated notes.
  • Ford Pro is a durable, high-margin commercial franchise that earned $6.8–9.0B of EBIT through the cycle.
  • The EV reset ends a $6.6B battery joint-venture commitment and cuts Model e losses toward about $4B.
  • Ford Credit is profitable and self-funding, and supports vehicle sales.

－ Risks

  • One S&P notch from losing its second investment-grade rating, which would require subsidiary guarantees and leave the notes structurally subordinated to bank lenders.
  • Thin margins: FY25 automotive EBIT margin of about 2.4%, below Moody's 4% downgrade trigger.
  • Warranty and recalls: the accrual is $17.7B, Ford is under an NHTSA consent order, and Ford discloses up to about $1.8B of possible costs above the accrual.
  • Net cash eroding: from $12.4B to $1.6B since FY22.
  • Tariff policy remains volatile, after about $2B of net cost in FY25.
  • Distributions reached 85% of adjusted FCF in FY25, above the 40–50% policy.

Recommendation

Positioning

Market Weight · fundamentals stable to improving

I would hold Ford Motor Co. senior unsecured notes at market weight rather than overweight. The liquidity and maturity profile make default risk remote over the next three years. However, the negative S&P outlook and the guarantee trigger make the notes vulnerable to a sudden loss in value if FY26 falls short. Until that outlook is resolved, the credit should trade at a spread premium to stable BBB automakers.

What would change the view

  • Upgrade to Overweight: FY26 adjusted EBIT at or above $10.5B, S&P outlook revised to stable, and distributions back within the 40–50% FCF policy.
  • Downgrade to Underweight: FY26 guidance cut below about $9B, a new large recall or tariff shock, or Company liquidity falling below about $35B.
  • What to watch: warranty cost per unit, Ford Pro margins, the timing of tariff reimbursements, Model e loss trajectory, and UAW talks ahead of the 2028 contract expiry.

Method

  • Segment data: FY23–25 segments use the FY25 10-K basis. FY22 segments use the FY23 10-K basis and exclude the former Ford Next segment (–$0.9B).
